0 → 1 Product Aug 2026 – Present

AnnaData

An SMS-first advisory agent for the feature phone a farmer already carries.

Problem
In 125+ farmer interviews, 80% had a smartphone in the house, but 0% used it for farming. The phone belonged to someone else, and the apps assumed a literacy the farmer didn't have.
Decision
Ship on SMS instead of an app. Ground every pesticide recommendation in 2,456 registered uses across 331 crops, parsed from 6 statutory CIB&RC registers. Distribute through KVK agronomists, not ads.
Result
MVP live with 100+ users, giving personalised crop-stage guidance in ~16 s. 5+ agronomist partners open ICAR centres of ~4,300 farmers each as low-CAC channels.

Bhakti Tradition Map

Interactive spatial–temporal visualisation of Bhakti saint networks · Under Dr. Priyadarshi Patnaik, Dept. of Humanities & Social Sciences, IIT Kharagpur · Nov 2025

1,300Years mapped (600–1900 CE)
8Filter dimensions
6REST endpoints
<150msAvg. API response

The Bhakti movement spans roughly 600–1900 CE and five major traditions: Vaishnava, Shaiva, Sant, Sufi and Shakta. The scholarship on it is excellent but scattered across static maps, linear chronologies and biographical records in a dozen languages. You can read about Kabir, or about the Alvars, but you can't easily see how devotional traditions moved across the subcontinent, or which lineages clustered where and when.

I built a web platform that answers that question interactively. It's a three-tier system where a researcher can filter 1,300 years of saint records by tradition, century, gender, language or philosophical school and watch the map redraw in under 250 ms.

Hardest problem

Historical uncertainty. Many saints have disputed or unknown dates, and the same place appears under three spellings across sources. Rather than claim a precision the sources don't support, I modelled period ranges instead of exact dates and relied on Nominatim's variant handling for place names. That was a data-modelling decision, not a coding one.

Engineering decisions

  • Vanilla JS over React. No build step, a smaller bundle, and direct DOM control for Leaflet markers, chosen so a future student maintainer could pick it up without toolchain overhead.
  • MongoDB over a relational store. Saint records are very uneven: some have precise dates and samadhi coordinates, most don't. A document schema with optional fields models that honestly instead of forcing nulls into rigid columns.
  • Marker clustering with chunked loading. Rendering hundreds of individual points was slow, so I clustered at a 50px radius and de-clustered progressively up to zoom 15 to keep interaction smooth.
  • Server-side geocoding via Nominatim. Contributors submit place names, not coordinates, so the API resolves them on submission, with caching and batching to respect rate limits.

Architecture

  • Frontend: ES6+ modules, Leaflet 1.9.4, markercluster 1.5.3, CartoDB tiles with light/dark/auto theming.
  • Backend: Node 18 + Express 4.19, MongoDB driver 6.18, CORS-restricted origins, server-side input validation.
  • Data: MongoDB Atlas, indexed on tradition, type, gender, language and startYear, plus a text index for search.
  • Deploy: Vercel for the static frontend and Render for the API, with environment-based endpoint switching.

Also built in

  • Community contribution flow with place autocomplete and validation.
  • Debounced live search (300 ms) across names, traditions and philosophy.
  • aria-live screen-reader announcements and a mobile-first responsive layout.
